How it works. There is no recipe list. Every ingredient is tagged with what it can be — a protein, a base, a vegetable, something to finish with — and a dozen meal shapes say how those slots fit together, so a stir fry is any protein, two or three of the right vegetables and a base, assembled from whatever you actually have. Each shape is scored against your fridge on the day it would be cooked: something going off tomorrow is worth roughly twenty times something that keeps a week. It only ever suggests a meal you can cook right now — nothing here will tell you to go shopping — so a thin fridge honestly gives you fewer meals rather than a list of things to buy. Cooking a meal takes its ingredients off the shelf before the next day is planned. Shelf lives are typical ones for an unopened item in a cold fridge — trust the label and your nose over this.
